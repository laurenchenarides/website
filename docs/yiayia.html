Yiayia’s Kitchen

Family recipes from my grandmother’s collection.

When I think of my yiayia (γιαγιά, Greek for grandmother), Mary, I picture her in the kitchen. She didn’t cook just because people needed feeding. I think she loved the kitchen because it was her craft, the place where she had the most freedom to create.

She was born in the 1920s, the second of five children of parents who had immigrated from Greece. I often wonder what it was like to be a child then, trying to find your place in a world that was so foreign to your parents. We learn so much from our parents without realizing it. I imagine she was lucky to discover a craft: partly passed down through tradition, partly because she was the eldest girl, and partly because she had a gift for hospitality.

These recipes come from her collection. Some are her own, and some came from the friends and family she cooked with: Ero’s kourabiedes, Vivian’s pumpkin pie, RoseAnn’s cheesecake, and a pie crust from Mrs. Myer’s mother in Tilson, New York.

I love it when I make something and think, “This tastes like what my yiayia would make.” I’m still working my way through these. Each recipe is marked Not yet tested until I’ve made it myself, so amounts and times may need adjusting. Every one has a printable card you can download.

Ero’s Kourabiedes

Greek butter cookies • Makes about 40

Not yet tested I haven't tested this one yet, so amounts and times may need adjusting.

From Ero, in Yiayia's recipe collection

Download recipe card (PDF)↑ All recipes
Oven350°F (175°C)
Prep15 mins
Bake15–20 mins per batch
MakesAbout 40 cookies

Ingredients

  • 1 lb (4 sticks) sweet (unsalted) butter, softened
  • 1 demitasse cup confectioners' sugar (about ½ cup)
  • 1 egg yolk, beaten
  • 1 jigger whiskey, cognac, or ouzo (about 1½ oz)
  • 5 cups all-purpose flour (Pillsbury recommended)
  • Confectioners' sugar, for finishing
  • Rose water, for brushing (optional)

Instructions

  1. Preheat the oven to 350°F (175°C). Line baking sheets with parchment paper.
Dough
  1. Beat the butter until creamy and light, like whipped cream.
  2. Gradually beat in the confectioners' sugar, then the egg yolk, until smooth and fluffy.
  3. Stir in the whiskey, cognac, or ouzo.
  4. Gradually add the flour until the dough is soft but not sticky. You may need a little more or less depending on humidity and your flour.
Shape & bake
  1. Shape walnut-size pieces into crescents or balls and set them about 1 inch apart on the sheets.
  2. Bake 15–20 minutes, until just starting to turn golden around the edges.
Finish
  1. While still warm, lightly brush with rose water (if using) and sift confectioners' sugar generously over the top.
  2. Cool a few minutes on the sheets, then move to a rack to cool completely. Sift on more sugar before serving.
Serving

Perfect for holidays and special occasions, and as gifts. Serve on a dessert platter with coffee or tea.

Galaktoboureko

Custard in phyllo with light syrup • Serves 8–10

Not yet tested I haven't tested this one yet, so amounts and times may need adjusting.

From Yiayia's recipe collection

Download recipe card (PDF)↑ All recipes
Oven350°F (175°C)
Prep30 mins
Bake45–55 mins
Serves8–10
Best guess: Yiayia's notes call this a phyllo pastry but leave the phyllo out. I've added it the traditional way, with sheets under and over the custard. Her custard uses cream of rice where many recipes use semolina, and it works the same way. If she topped it with shredded phyllo (kataifi) instead, see the note at the end.

Ingredients

Custard
  • ¼ cup (½ stick) butter
  • 3 cups milk
  • ¾ cup sugar
  • 1 cup cream of rice (or fine semolina)
  • 1 tsp vanilla extract
  • 4 large eggs (or 5 if small), beaten
Phyllo (best guess)
  • 1 lb (16 oz) phyllo sheets, thawed
  • ¾–1 cup (1½–2 sticks) butter, melted, for brushing
Light syrup
  • 1 cup water
  • ½ cup sugar
  • ½ tsp lemon juice

Instructions

Custard
  1. In a large saucepan, heat the milk, butter, and sugar over medium heat until nearly boiling.
  2. Slowly whisk in the cream of rice and keep whisking until thick and smooth.
  3. Remove from the heat and cool to lukewarm, stirring now and then so a skin doesn't form.
  4. Gradually whisk in the beaten eggs until smooth and creamy, then stir in the vanilla.
Syrup
  1. In a small saucepan, bring the water, sugar, and lemon juice to a boil. Simmer until slightly thickened, about 10 minutes. Cool completely.
Bake & finish
  1. Preheat the oven to 350°F (175°C). Butter a 9×13-inch baking dish.
  2. Layer about half the phyllo sheets in the dish, brushing each with melted butter and letting the edges hang over the sides. Keep the rest covered with a damp towel so it doesn't dry out.
  3. Pour in the custard and smooth the top. Fold the overhanging phyllo over it.
  4. Layer the remaining sheets on top, brushing each with butter. Tuck in the edges, then score the top layers into squares with a sharp knife, without cutting down to the custard.
  5. Bake 45–55 minutes, until the phyllo is deep golden and the custard is set.
  6. Let it cool slightly, then pour the cooled syrup evenly over the top so it soaks in.
  7. Cool completely before cutting along the scored lines.
Serving

Serve chilled or at room temperature. It pairs beautifully with a cup of Greek coffee.

Shredded phyllo top

If Yiayia meant phyllo shreds on top, use 8 oz kataifi instead of the top sheets. Pull it apart, toss it with ½ cup melted butter, and spread it over the custard before baking.

Vasilopita

Greek New Year bread with a hidden coin

Not yet tested I haven't tested this one yet, so amounts and times may need adjusting.

From Yiayia's recipe collection

Download recipe card (PDF)↑ All recipes
Oven325°F (165°C)
Rise2 × 1 hr
Bake35–45 mins, to 190°F
ForNew Year's Day
Doneness: The family notes say about 20 minutes, but a loaf made with 4 cups of flour usually needs 35–45 minutes at 325°F. Go by temperature, not time: it's done when the center reads 190°F on an instant-read thermometer (about 185°F at high altitude, where water boils at a lower temperature). If the top browns before the inside is done, tent it loosely with foil. The notes sweeten it with Equal packets plus powdered sugar.

Ingredients

Bread
  • ½ cup water (for steeping the spices)
  • ¼ tsp mastic (mastiha), ground
  • ¼ tsp mahlepi (mahlab), ground
  • ¼ lb (1 stick) salted butter, melted
  • ⅓ cup sweetened condensed milk, warmed
  • 3 eggs, warmed
  • 4 cups flour
  • ½ cup confectioners' (powdered) sugar
  • 4 packets Equal sweetener
  • 2 packages instant yeast
  • 1 dime (or other coin), wrapped in aluminum foil
Glaze
  • 1 egg yolk
  • 1 tbsp water
  • Sesame seeds, for sprinkling

Instructions

Spiced liquid
  1. Bring the ½ cup water to a boil in a small pot, add the mastic and mahlepi, and remove from the heat.
  2. Add the melted butter and condensed milk. Cool to room temperature, then whisk in the warm eggs.
Dough
  1. Sift the flour into a large bowl and mix in the powdered sugar, Equal, and yeast.
  2. Make a well in the center, pour in the liquid, and knead until the dough is soft and not sticky. (Buttered, gloved hands help.)
  3. Cover with a towel and let rise at room temperature for 1 hour.
Shape & bake
  1. Knead briefly, shape as you like, and press the foil-wrapped coin into the dough.
  2. Mix the egg yolk and water, brush it over the top, and sprinkle with sesame seeds.
  3. Let rise another hour at room temperature.
  4. Bake at 325°F (165°C) until deep golden and the center reads 190°F, about 35–45 minutes. Start checking at 20 minutes (see the note above).
  5. Cool before slicing. Whoever finds the coin has good luck for the year!
Using a KitchenAid

Mix the dry ingredients with the flat beater. Switch to the dough hook and add the liquid on low (speed 2), then knead on medium (speed 4–6) for 8–10 minutes until soft, smooth, elastic, and clear of the bowl. Let it rise in a greased, covered bowl until doubled, about 1 hour. Shape, set on a parchment-lined sheet, cover, and rise another hour.

Vivian’s Pumpkin Pie

Two pies, with a touch of molasses • 8–10 servings each

Not yet tested I haven't tested this one yet, so amounts and times may need adjusting.

From Vivian, in Yiayia's recipe collection

Download recipe card (PDF)↑ All recipes
Oven425°F, then 350°F
BakeAbout 60 mins
Makes2 pies
Crust2 unbaked shells

Ingredients

  • 4 eggs, slightly beaten
  • 1 can (29 oz) pumpkin purée (not pie filling)
  • 1½ cups granulated sugar
  • ¾ tsp salt
  • ½ tsp ground cinnamon, plus extra for sprinkling
  • 1 tsp pumpkin pie spice
  • 1 tsp ground ginger
  • 2 cans (13 oz each) evaporated milk, or 3½ cups half-and-half
  • ⅓ cup molasses
  • 2 unbaked pie shells (try the Pie Crust under Basics)

Instructions

  1. Preheat the oven to 425°F (220°C).
  2. In a large bowl, mix the eggs, pumpkin, sugar, salt, cinnamon, pumpkin pie spice, and ginger.
  3. Gradually stir in the evaporated milk or half-and-half, then the molasses, until fully combined.
  4. Divide the filling between the two pie shells.
  5. Bake 15 minutes at 425°F, then lower the oven to 350°F (175°C) and bake about 45 minutes more. Halfway through, sprinkle the tops with cinnamon.
  6. The pies are done when a knife inserted near the center comes out clean. Cool on a wire rack.
Swap

Cooked, mashed butternut squash can stand in for the pumpkin.

Serving

Serve at room temperature or chilled, with whipped cream or vanilla ice cream.

RoseAnn’s Cheese Cake

Ricotta and cream cheese • Optional berry glaze • Serves 8–10

Not yet tested I haven't tested this one yet, so amounts and times may need adjusting.

From RoseAnn, in Yiayia's recipe collection

Download recipe card (PDF)↑ All recipes
Oven325°F (163°C)
Bake1 hr + 2 hrs in the closed oven
PanSpringform, greased
Serves8–10

Ingredients

Cheesecake
  • 1 lb whole-milk ricotta
  • 1 lb cream cheese
  • 1½ cups granulated sugar
  • 4 large eggs
  • 3 drops lemon juice
  • 1 tsp vanilla extract
  • 5 tbsp cornstarch
  • 3 tbsp all-purpose flour
  • ¼ lb (1 stick) butter, melted over hot water
  • 1 pint (16 oz) sour cream
Glaze (optional)
  • ½ cup liquid (juice from the berries, topped up with water)
  • ¼ cup granulated sugar
  • 1 tsp lemon juice
  • 1 tbsp cornstarch
  • Cherries or blueberries, for topping

Instructions

Cheesecake
  1. Preheat the oven to 325°F (163°C) and grease a springform pan.
  2. Beat the ricotta and cream cheese until smooth. Gradually add the sugar.
  3. Add the eggs one at a time, beating well after each, then the lemon juice and vanilla.
  4. Whisk the cornstarch and flour together and mix them in.
  5. Mix in the melted butter and sour cream until combined, then pour into the pan.
  6. Bake 1 hour, then turn the oven off and leave the cheesecake in the closed oven for 2 more hours without opening the door.
  7. Remove, cool completely, and refrigerate until serving.
Glaze (optional)
  1. In a saucepan over medium heat, cook the liquid, sugar, lemon juice, and cornstarch, stirring constantly, until thickened. Cool slightly.
  2. Fold in the berries (or just top the cheesecake with them) and spread over the chilled cheesecake before serving.

7-Up Cake

Cake mix + pudding + 7-Up • Bundt or tube pan • Serves 8–10

Not yet tested I haven't tested this one yet, so amounts and times may need adjusting.

From Yiayia's recipe collection

Download recipe card (PDF)↑ All recipes
Oven350°F (175°C)
BakeAbout 45 mins
PanTube or bundt, greased
Serves8–10

Ingredients

  • 1 box lemon or yellow cake mix (preferably Duncan Hines)
  • 1 box instant pudding mix (lemon or vanilla)
  • ½ cup vegetable oil
  • 1 cup 7-Up or ginger ale
  • 1 tsp vanilla extract
  • 4 large eggs
  • 1 cup chopped nuts (walnuts recommended)

Instructions

  1. Preheat the oven to 350°F (175°C) and grease a tube or bundt pan.
  2. In a large bowl, combine the cake mix and pudding mix.
  3. Add the oil, 7-Up, vanilla, and eggs, and beat with an electric mixer until smooth.
  4. Fold in the nuts and pour the batter into the pan.
  5. Bake about 45 minutes, until a toothpick in the center comes out clean.
  6. Cool in the pan 10 minutes, then turn out onto a rack to cool completely.
Serving

Serve as is or with a light dusting of powdered sugar.

Jelly Cookies (Cut-Outs)

Sandwich cookies with jelly • Makes about 24

Not yet tested I haven't tested this one yet, so amounts and times may need adjusting.

From Yiayia's recipe collection

Download recipe card (PDF)↑ All recipes
Oven350°F (175°C)
Bake8–10 mins
Roll¼ inch thick
MakesAbout 24
Best guess: The notes don't list any flour. This dough (butter, cream, and no sugar) matches a classic cream wafer, which uses about 2 cups of flour per cup of butter. With the extra cream here, start with 2 cups and add up to ¼ cup more until the dough rolls without sticking. Note the amount you use.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup butter, softened
  • ½ cup light cream
  • 1 egg yolk
  • 1 tsp baking powder
  • 2–2¼ cups all-purpose flour (best guess; see above)
  • Powdered sugar, for dusting
  • About ½ cup jelly (any flavor), for filling

Instructions

Dough
  1. Preheat the oven to 350°F (175°C). Line a baking sheet with parchment (or use a nonstick pan).
  2. Cream the butter and light cream until smooth. Mix in the egg yolk, then the baking powder.
  3. Mix in 2 cups of flour. If the dough is still sticky, add more a tablespoon at a time. If it's soft, chill it for 30 minutes before rolling.
Cut & bake
  1. On a lightly floured surface, roll the dough about ¼ inch thick and cut shapes. Cut an even number: each cookie needs a top and a bottom.
  2. Bake 8–10 minutes, until the edges just start to brown. Cool a few minutes on the sheet, then on a rack.
Assemble
  1. Dust half the cookies (the tops) with powdered sugar. Spread a little jelly in the center of the rest (the bottoms).
  2. Press the tops onto the bottoms. Let the jelly set slightly before serving, or store airtight.

Rolled Florettes

Rolled jelly-nut cookies • Makes about 24

Not yet tested I haven't tested this one yet, so amounts and times may need adjusting.

L. Tsontarides, September 1971, in Yiayia's recipe collection

Download recipe card (PDF)↑ All recipes
Oven350°F (175°C)
Bake12–15 mins
Roll¼ inch thick
MakesAbout 24

Ingredients

Dough
  • 1 cup butter, softened
  • ½ cup light cream, room temp
  • ½ tsp yeast, dissolved in the cream
  • 1 egg yolk
  • 1 tsp baking powder
  • Flour, as needed for a soft, rollable dough
Filling
  • 1 cup jam or preserves (any flavor)
  • ½ cup chopped nuts
  • ¼ tsp nutmeg
  • 2 tbsp Zwieback crumbs (or crushed plain biscuits or breadcrumbs)
Finish
  • Confectioners' sugar, for dusting

Instructions

Dough & filling
  1. Cream the butter with the cream and dissolved yeast. Mix in the egg yolk.
  2. Mix in the baking powder and enough flour to make a soft, rollable dough.
  3. In another bowl, mix the jam, nuts, nutmeg, and Zwieback crumbs.
Roll, slice & bake
  1. Preheat the oven to 350°F (175°C) and butter a baking sheet.
  2. On a floured surface, roll the dough about ¼ inch thick and spread the filling evenly over it.
  3. Roll it up into a log and cut diagonally into slices about 1 inch thick.
  4. Bake 12–15 minutes, until lightly golden. Cool a few minutes on the sheet, then on a rack.
  5. Dust with confectioners' sugar once cool.

Apricot Jello Mold

Layered apricot gelatin with sour cream • Serves 6–8

Not yet tested I haven't tested this one yet, so amounts and times may need adjusting.

From Yiayia's recipe collection

Download recipe card (PDF)↑ All recipes
ChillUntil fully set (best overnight)
MoldGreased (or nonstick Tupperware)
Serves6–8
MakeA day ahead

Ingredients

  • 2 small boxes apricot gelatin
  • 2 cans (12 oz each) apricot nectar
  • 1 can (5.5 oz) evaporated milk
  • ½ pint sour cream
  • 1 cup warm water

Instructions

First layer
  1. In a saucepan, dissolve one box of gelatin in the 1 cup warm water. Remove from the heat and stir in 1 cup apricot nectar.
  2. Pour into a greased mold and refrigerate until fully set.
Sour cream layer
  1. Spread the sour cream evenly over the set gelatin and return it to the fridge.
Second layer
  1. Dissolve the second box of gelatin in 1 cup apricot nectar (no heat). Let it come to room temperature, then stir in the evaporated milk.
  2. Pour gently over the sour cream layer and refrigerate until the whole mold is firm.

Stuffed Zucchini

Vegetable, quinoa & Parmesan filling • Serves 5

Not yet tested I haven't tested this one yet, so amounts and times may need adjusting.

From Yiayia's recipe collection

Download recipe card (PDF)↑ All recipes
Oven375°F (190°C)
Prep20 mins
Bake30 mins
Serves5 (1 zucchini each)

Ingredients

  • 5 medium zucchini
  • 3 tbsp olive oil
  • 3 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 large onion, finely chopped
  • 1 red bell pepper, finely chopped
  • ½ cup chopped celery
  • ½ cup chopped mushrooms
  • ½ cup cooked quinoa
  • Zucchini pulp, chopped (saved from scooping)
  • ¼ cup grated Parmesan
  • Salt and pepper, to taste

Instructions

  1. Preheat the oven to 375°F (190°C).
  2. Halve the zucchini lengthwise and scoop out the pulp, leaving a ¼-inch shell. Chop the pulp and set it aside.
  3. Heat the oil in a large skillet over medium heat. Sauté the garlic, onion, and red pepper until the onion is translucent, about 5 minutes.
  4. Add the celery and mushrooms and cook until tender, about 5 minutes more.
  5. Stir in the zucchini pulp and quinoa and cook 2–3 minutes. Season with salt and pepper.
  6. Remove from the heat, cool slightly, and stir in the Parmesan.
  7. Arrange the shells on a baking sheet and spoon in the filling, packing lightly.
  8. Bake 30 minutes, until the shells are tender and the tops lightly browned. Serve warm, with extra Parmesan or a drizzle of olive oil.

Couscous Salad

Artichokes, chickpeas, mint & chicken • Serves 6–8

Not yet tested I haven't tested this one yet, so amounts and times may need adjusting.

From Yiayia's recipe collection

Download recipe card (PDF)↑ All recipes
PrepAbout 20 mins
Chill30 mins+
Serves6–8
ServeCold

Ingredients

  • 1 cup couscous
  • 1 cup vegetable broth
  • 1 can (about 14 oz) artichoke hearts, drained and chopped
  • 1 bunch scallions, chopped
  • ¼ cup fresh mint, chopped
  • 1 large carton (about 15 oz) chickpeas, drained and rinsed
  • 1 cup cherry tomatoes, halved
  • ½ English cucumber, chopped
  • 1 cooked chicken breast, chopped
  • ½ cup olive oil
  • Juice of ½ lemon
  • ½ tsp cayenne pepper (to taste)

Instructions

  1. Bring the broth to a boil, stir in the couscous, and remove from the heat. Cover for 5 minutes, until the liquid is absorbed. Fluff with a fork and cool.
  2. In a large bowl, combine the couscous, artichokes, scallions, mint, chickpeas, tomatoes, and cucumber.
  3. Gently mix in the chicken. Drizzle with the olive oil and lemon juice, sprinkle with cayenne, and mix well.
  4. Chill at least 30 minutes. Stir again before serving and adjust the lemon or cayenne to taste.
Vegetarian

The notes filed this under vegetarian dishes, but it includes chicken. Leave the chicken out for a vegetarian version.

Sausage Maple Hash with Sweet Potatoes

With yellow tomato salsa and a melon-cucumber salad • Serves 4

Not yet tested I haven't tested this one yet, so amounts and times may need adjusting.

From Yiayia's recipe collection

Download recipe card (PDF)↑ All recipes
PanLarge skillet
Serves4
PartsHash, sauce, salad
HeatMedium

Ingredients

Hash
  • 1 lb maple-flavored sausage
  • 2 medium sweet potatoes, peeled and diced
  • 1 large red onion, diced
  • 1 tsp chili powder
  • 1 tsp smoked cumin
  • ½ tsp ground coriander
  • ½ cup grated Manchego
Sauce
  • 3 yellow tomatoes
  • 1 small jalapeño, seeded and finely chopped
  • 1 small red onion, finely chopped
  • ¼ cup chopped fresh cilantro
  • Juice of 1 lime
Salad
  • 1 cup diced melon (any kind)
  • 1 English cucumber, diced, skin on
  • ¼ cup chopped red onion
  • ½ cup sliced radishes
Dressing
  • 2 tbsp mint jelly
  • 3 tbsp olive oil
  • Juice of 1 lime

Instructions

Hash
  1. In a large skillet over medium heat, cook the sausage, breaking it up, until cooked through and slightly crispy.
  2. Add the sweet potatoes and red onion and cook, stirring now and then, until the potatoes are tender and lightly browned.
  3. Stir in the chili powder, smoked cumin, and coriander and cook 2 minutes more.
  4. Off the heat, sprinkle the Manchego over the hot hash and let it melt.
Sauce
  1. Blanch the tomatoes to slip off the skins, remove the seeds, and dice.
  2. Mix the tomatoes with the jalapeño, red onion, cilantro, and lime juice. Set aside to let the flavors meld.
Salad & dressing
  1. Combine the melon, cucumber, red onion, and radishes.
  2. Whisk the mint jelly, olive oil, and lime juice, then toss with the salad.
Serve
  1. Divide the hash among plates, top with a spoonful of the sauce, and serve the salad on the side.

Skordalia

Greek garlic sauce, two ways

Not yet tested I haven't tested this one yet, so amounts and times may need adjusting.

From Yiayia's recipe collection

Download recipe card (PDF)↑ All recipes
  • Blender

Quick blender version • About 1 cup

Prep15 mins
Chill1 hr for best flavor
MakesAbout 1 cup
ToolFood processor or blender

Ingredients

  • 1 medium clove garlic
  • ¼ cup vinegar (white or red wine vinegar preferred)
  • ½ cup olive oil
  • 8 slices white bread, crusts removed
  • Water, for soaking the bread

Instructions

  1. Soak the bread in water just until moistened, then squeeze out the excess thoroughly. It should be damp, not wet.
  2. In a food processor or blender, pulse the bread, garlic, and vinegar into a smooth paste.
  3. With the machine running, drizzle in the olive oil very slowly so it emulsifies and turns creamy.
  4. Thin with a little water or vinegar if needed, and adjust the garlic and vinegar to taste.
  5. Chill at least an hour to let the flavors meld. Serve chilled or at room temperature.
Serving

Serve as a dip with vegetables or fish, as a spread for bread, or on a meze platter alongside fried zucchini and eggplant.

Whole-head garlic version • About 1–1½ cups

Prep20 mins
MakesAbout 1–1½ cups
Bread1–2 days old
ToolMortar & pestle, mixer

Ingredients

  • 1 whole head garlic, cloves peeled
  • 1 tsp salt
  • ¼ cup white wine or cider vinegar
  • 1 large loaf dried bread (a day or two old)
  • About 1 cup olive oil

Instructions

  1. Mash the garlic with the salt in a mortar and pestle until smooth and pasty. (No mortar? Use the flat of a knife or a small food processor.)
  2. Cut off the bread crusts. Soak the soft center just until moistened, then squeeze out all the excess water.
  3. Put the bread in a large bowl with the garlic paste and vinegar.
  4. Beat with an electric mixer or a strong whisk while drizzling in the olive oil very slowly, until thick and creamy like a thick mayonnaise.
  5. Adjust the salt and vinegar to taste, and loosen with a little vinegar or water if it's too thick.
Serving

Serve with vegetables, grilled meats, or warm bread, or as part of a Greek meze platter.

Balsamic and Crème Fraîche Fruit Sauce

A rich drizzle for fresh fruit

Not yet tested I haven't tested this one yet, so amounts and times may need adjusting.

From Yiayia's recipe collection

Prep5 mins
Cook10 mins
ReduceBy about half
ServeOver fresh fruit

Ingredients

  • ¾ cup balsamic vinegar
  • 1 heaping tbsp crème fraîche
  • 1 tsp sugar

Instructions

  1. In a small saucepan over medium heat, whisk the vinegar, crème fraîche, and sugar together.
  2. Bring to a boil, stirring so the sugar dissolves.
  3. Lower the heat and simmer, stirring occasionally, until reduced by about half, 5–10 minutes.
  4. Cool slightly and drizzle sparingly over fresh fruit; the flavor is rich.
Serving

Lovely with strawberries, blueberries, or sliced pears, on a dessert plate or in a fruit salad.

Shortening crust • 1 double or 2 single crusts

Makes1 double or 2 single
Chill30 mins+
FatCrisco (shortening)
BakePer your pie recipe

Ingredients

  • 2 cups all-purpose flour
  • 1 tsp salt
  • ¾ cup Crisco (vegetable shortening)
  • 4–6 tbsp ice water

Instructions

  1. In a large bowl, mix the flour and salt.
  2. Cut in the Crisco with a pastry cutter or two forks until it looks like coarse crumbs with some pea-size pieces.
  3. Sprinkle 4 tablespoons ice water over it and mix gently with a fork, adding more a tablespoon at a time just until the dough starts to come together.
  4. Turn it out onto a lightly floured surface and knead a few times into a ball. Divide in half for two single crusts.
  5. Flatten into discs, wrap, and chill at least 30 minutes.
  6. To use, let a well-chilled disc sit about 10 minutes, roll it out on a floured surface to fit your dish, and bake as your pie recipe directs.
